## Three changes to ALAGA, drawn from your map

**Change 1 — sell quarterly, not monthly.** Pet subscription churn benchmarks:
monthly plans churn at **18–22% annually**, quarterly at **11–16%**, annual at
**6–9%**. Operators report that extending billing cadence cuts churn *without*
raising acquisition cost. A joint supplement is a 30–60 day supply, so a quarterly
box is the natural unit anyway. Launch quarterly-default, monthly as the opt-out.
This is free retention.

**Change 2 — I need to refine what I told you on Monday.** I said repeat rate does
not decide payback, and within a 24-month window that holds — the ramp is driven by
new customers. But your map thinks in exit multiples, and on that horizon retention
*is* the valuation. A pet supplement brand transacts at a premium EV/EBITDA
precisely because of repeat purchase. So: repeat rate does not move your payback
date, and it substantially determines what the business is worth when you sell it.
Both are true; I framed only the first.

**Change 3 — lead with the registration.** Per your snake-oil note: "BAI-registered"
and the stability data belong on the front of the pack and in the first line of
every listing. Competitors on Shopee cannot say it. That is the differentiation,
and it is more durable than the 30-day guarantee.

## New candidates from your map

### A. Fragrance / body mist, Philippine TikTok — strongest new PH-scale idea

This fits your stated capital range better than anything since the pet play, and it
is **faster to market than ALAGA**.

- **Regulatory is materially lighter than supplements.** Perfume is a cosmetic in
  the Philippines: you need a License to Operate plus a Cosmetic Product
  Notification, and the process runs **up to 3 months** — against 6–12 for BAI
  supplement registration. Same documentation muscle (GMP, certificate of free
  sale), a third of the wait. Note a foreign company cannot notify directly; you
  need a Philippine-based Market Authorisation Holder, which you would be.
- **The trend is a multi-unit purchase, not a single sale.** Gen Z owns 8–12
  scents and layers them. Body mists are the affordable entry point that drives the
  layering behaviour — lower price, higher frequency, exactly the volume dynamic
  TikTok Shop rewards.
- **Channel fit is close to perfect.** PH TikTok Shop has ~78m users, average order
  ~₱850, and beauty & personal care is a top category. Your own note — *upper-mass
  price point is the sweet spot, hybrid distribution, pure D2C rarely sustainable* —
  describes precisely this play.
- Light, high margin, no cold chain, and **no electricity** — which matters in the
  month Meralco hit ₱14.83/kWh.

**Against it:** low barriers to entry, and your own fragrance note that pure D2C
does not sustain — you would need a retail path within 18 months.

### B. GLP-1 companion and post-GLP-1 — highest conviction if the frame is UK

You flagged this and you are right that it is the interesting space.

- **GLP-1 prescriptions are up ~587% in five years.**
- **Roughly 100% of patients experience nausea**; muscle loss, fatigue and
  constipation are common.
- **About half of adults stop within a year.** That is the post-GLP-1 transition
  market you identified — and it is not a niche, it is half the cohort.
- The supplement stack is already well-defined by clinicians: protein, multivitamin,
  magnesium, electrolytes, omega-3, and for muscle preservation creatine and HMB.

Two honest cautions. This is a **UK/US-scale play, not a ₱2.5M one** — it needs
clinical credibility, regulatory care around claims, and marketing spend. And the
"post-GLP-1" positioning depends on people churning off the drugs continuing at
current rates; if next-generation orals improve tolerability, the transition
cohort shrinks. Worth watching quarterly.

### C. Female health in supplements — the gap you named, unexplored

Your map flags female health as underserved in supplements, and separately notes
women's sport is fragmented. I have not scanned either. Flagging it as the most
promising unexamined thread in your map, for the next scan.
