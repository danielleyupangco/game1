01

The business model

Buy a proven formulation from a certified contract manufacturer, put a Philippine brand and a government registration on it, and sell it to owners who reorder every quarter. The margin comes from the import gap; the defensibility comes from the registration; the value comes from the reorder.

Source

Private label, not manufacture

A GMP-certified factory makes the chews to a standard formulation. No plant, no R&D, no staff beyond a manager.

Defend

BAI registration

The dossier — free-sale certificate, GMP, tropical stability data — takes 6–12 months and real money. That barrier is the moat.

Sell

Direct + salon wholesale

70% through Shopee, TikTok Shop and Instagram. 30% wholesale to grooming salons, which are the real pet-health touchpoint here.

Keep

Quarterly subscription

Billed every three months, not monthly. Same product, materially lower churn, no extra acquisition cost.

Why joint, and why dogs. Mobility change is visible to the owner within two to four weeks. A product whose effect the customer can see does its own marketing — and it is the only honest way to earn a reorder in a category buyers distrust.

06

Kill criteria

Agreed in advance, so the decision is made once — now — rather than argued with yourself later when money is already committed.

Stop or renegotiate if any of these is true

  • Landed cost exceeds ₱600 per jar. The margin structure that makes this work stops working.
  • Cohort repeat conversion is below 15% at month 4. Without the reorder there is no business, only a series of expensive first sales.
  • Returns or spoilage exceed 8%. Soft chews failing in tropical humidity — a formulation or packaging problem, not a marketing one.
  • BAI registration quotes above ₱500,000 per SKU or beyond 12 months. The moat costs more than the territory is worth.
  • No supplier can produce a Certificate of Free Sale and Zone IVb stability data at an MOQ under ₱300,000. Without the dossier there is no legal product.
  • The peso passes ₱67 and holds. Not fatal — margin only falls to about 75% — but reprice deliberately rather than absorbing it.
