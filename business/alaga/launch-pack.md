# ALAGA — Launch Pack, SKU #1

Dog joint supplement. One SKU, taken all the way, before anything else is added.

---

## 1. The brand

**Name: ALAGA** — Filipino for the one you care for; as a verb, to care for.

It is the right name for three reasons. It is instantly understood by every
Filipino buyer without translation. It carries warmth without being cute, which
matters for a product making a health claim. And it extends cleanly across a
range — Alaga Flex, Alaga Calm, Alaga Vital — so the second and third SKUs do
not need a new brand built from scratch.

**⚠ Before spending anything on artwork:** run a trademark search at IPOPHL
(Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines) for "ALAGA" in Class 5
(veterinary preparations and dietary supplements for animals) and Class 31
(foodstuffs for animals). It is a common Filipino word, so registrability is not
guaranteed. If it is blocked, the fallback names below are deliberately more
coinable and therefore easier to register.

Fallbacks, in order: **ALAGAVET** · **KALINGA** (tender care) · **LAKAS** (strength,
narrower but strong for joint specifically).

**Product name: ALAGA FLEX** — joint and mobility support for dogs.

**Positioning line:** *Supplements you can see working.*

**The promise that drives everything:** a **30-day visible-results guarantee** —
full refund if the owner sees no change in their dog's mobility within 30 days.

This is the strategic centre of the brand, not a marketing flourish. It does three
jobs at once. It forces you to select a genuinely efficacious formulation, because
a weak one becomes expensive fast. It converts hesitant first-time buyers, which
is the hardest step in supplements. And it produces the word-of-mouth that makes
the product market itself — the entire reason this category was chosen over cat
food and wearables.

Model the refund exposure honestly: the financial model carries a 5% returns and
spoilage allowance, and the kill criterion is 8%. Track it weekly from launch.

**Who it is for:** Metro Manila dog owners, 28–50, condo and village households,
who already spend on grooming and premium food and treat the dog as family. They
buy on Shopee, TikTok Shop and Instagram, and they ask their groomer for advice
more often than they see a vet — because there is roughly one vet per 10,000
pet owners in the country.

**Visual direction:** clinical warmth. Off-white, deep forest green, a single warm
accent. Clear typography, dosage and actives legible on the front of pack, real
dog photography rather than illustration. It should look like something a vet
would hand you, not like a treat. Every competitor on Shopee looks like a treat —
that gap is the shelf-level differentiation.

## 2. Supplier target list

### Lane A — United States / NASC certified (recommended first)

These matter because a US FDA-registered facility can obtain a **Certificate of
Free Sale**, which is precisely the document the Bureau of Animal Industry
requires in your registration dossier. Higher FOB, higher MOQ, materially easier
registration — and "Made in USA" supports the premium price point.

## 3. First 90 days

**Weeks 1–2 — set the foundation**
- IPOPHL trademark search on ALAGA, Classes 5 and 31. If clear, file.
- Register the business: DTI business name, BIR, Makati LGU permit.
- Open the Gmail alias you will use for supplier correspondence.
- Send the RFQ to all eleven suppliers above. Log replies in the scoring table.

**Weeks 3–5 — narrow and sample**
- Score every reply. Disqualify anyone failing the three starred criteria.
- Order samples from the top three. Budget ₱30–60K including courier.
- Engage a **BAI regulatory consultant** in parallel — do not wait for samples.
  Ask for a written quote and timeline for one product registration. This is the
  long pole in the whole plan; every week of delay here is a week of delayed launch.

**Weeks 6–9 — evaluate and decide**
- Palatability test the samples on real dogs, ideally 10–15 through a friendly
  grooming salon. Palatability failure is the commonest reason a chew does not repeat.
- Leave samples in a hot car and an un-airconditioned room for two weeks. If they
  soften, sweat oil, or mould, that supplier is finished regardless of price.
- Select the supplier. Negotiate PH exclusivity or priority pricing against volume.
- Commission brand identity and packaging artwork against the chosen pack format.

**Weeks 10–13 — commit**
- Place the first purchase order at MOQ.
- File the BAI dossier through the consultant.
- Build the Shopee and TikTok Shop storefronts; secure the Instagram handle.
- Line up 5–8 grooming salons for the wholesale pilot — not vet clinics, there
  are too few of them to scale through.
- Recruit the manager. The operating model is owner-with-manager: your job after
  this point is weekly review, not daily operations.

**What you are not doing in these 90 days:** launching a second SKU, buying
inventory beyond MOQ, or signing a lease. One SKU, taken all the way.

## 4. Where this sits against the rest of the plan

The grooming hard-goods line from the July 31 scan still runs first and funds
this. It needs no registration, so it can launch while the BAI dossier sits in
process for 6–12 months. Alaga Flex then launches into a customer base you
already own, through wholesale relationships you already have.

That sequencing is the whole point: the unregulated products buy the time that
the regulated, defensible product needs.

## 5. What the model says before any quotes arrive

Run independently in Python against the same assumptions, so these are the
workbook's own numbers, not a separate guess:

| | China lane | US / NASC lane |
|---|---|---|
| Landed cost per unit | ₱295 | ₱506 |
| Initial inventory (at MOQ) | ₱295,000 | ₱1,265,000 |
| Total capital required | ₱918,000 | ₱2,082,000 |
| Gross margin — direct | 77% | 61% |
| Gross margin — wholesale | 59% | **29% — under the 30% screen** |
| EBITDA turns positive | month 5 | month 8 |
| **Payback (base case)** | **month 15** | **beyond 24 months** |

**Two things this changes.**

First, at these placeholder prices the China lane wins on the math, and the US
lane fails the payback screen. My earlier reasoning — that US/NASC likely wins
because of the Certificate of Free Sale — holds on paperwork but not on economics.
The US lane only becomes competitive at roughly **FOB $5.00 or MOQ 1,500**, which
is exactly why the RFQ asks for tiered pricing at four volumes. Get the quotes
before choosing.

Second, and counter to what I said earlier: **repeat rate is not what decides
payback.** Moving repeat conversion from 10% to 45% shifts China payback only from
month 17 to month 15, because within 24 months the ramp is dominated by new
customer acquisition. Landed cost and MOQ are the real levers. Repeat rate still
governs long-run value and everything past year two — keep the 15% kill criterion
— but do not let it drive the supplier decision.

**The decision rule, restated:** if a China supplier can produce a Certificate of
Free Sale and Zone IVb stability data, take it. If none can, the real question
becomes whether to pay the US premium or have no registration at all — and that
is a question the RFQ replies will answer within three weeks.
