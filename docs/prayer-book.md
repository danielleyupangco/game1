# A daily prayer book for this pregnancy

Source content for the app's prayer section. Seeds a `prayers` table:
`slug`, `title`, `kind`, `week`, `season`, `body`, `source`.

**Compiled 18 September 2026.** For Dani, carrying through Advent, Christmas,
Lent and Easter, and due in the month of Mary.

## How this was put together

**Traditional prayers are given in full and unaltered.** The Hail Mary, the
Memorare, the *Sub Tuum Praesidium*, the Angelus, the Magnificat and the
Roman Ritual's blessing of a mother before childbirth are ancient and held in
common by the whole Church. They are marked `source: traditional`.

**Everything else was written for this pregnancy** — the weekly prayers, the
prayers for particular moments, the reflections. They are marked
`source: original`. They carry no ecclesiastical approval and claim none; they
are private devotion, in the ordinary sense that any family's prayers are.

**Nothing here is copied from a published devotional.** If Dani wants a book
with an imprimatur on the shelf beside this, that is a good instinct and this
is not a substitute for one.

> **A note on what prayer is not.** This book sits beside the medical care, not
> in place of it. Praying about a symptom and calling Dra. Villafria about it
> are not alternatives. The red-flags screen is one tap away for a reason.

## 1. The daily rhythm

Four short prayers, one for each hinge of the day. The whole cycle takes under
five minutes. **It is meant to be sustainable at 3am in the third trimester**,
which is the only test that matters.

### On waking — the morning offering
*`kind: daily`, `source: original`*

> Lord, this day is yours, and so is the child you are forming in me.
>
> I offer you the ordinary things — the tiredness, the work, the meals I may
> not manage to eat. Take the small discomforts and make them count for
> something.
>
> Keep our cub safe today. Keep me steady. And when I forget to think of you,
> go on holding us anyway.
>
> Amen.

### At midday — the Angelus
*`kind: daily`, `source: traditional`*

Traditionally prayed at six, noon and six, and the most fitting of all prayers
for a pregnant woman: it is the Incarnation, recounted three times a day.

> **V.** The Angel of the Lord declared unto Mary,
> **R.** And she conceived of the Holy Spirit.
> *Hail Mary…*
>
> **V.** Behold the handmaid of the Lord,
> **R.** Be it done unto me according to thy word.
> *Hail Mary…*
>
> **V.** And the Word was made Flesh,
> **R.** And dwelt among us.
> *Hail Mary…*
>
> **V.** Pray for us, O holy Mother of God,
> **R.** That we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ.
>
> Let us pray. Pour forth, we beseech thee, O Lord, thy grace into our hearts;
> that we, to whom the Incarnation of Christ thy Son was made known by the
> message of an angel, may by his Passion and Cross be brought to the glory of
> his Resurrection. Through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

**Why this one.** Mary was pregnant. Not symbolically — actually, bodily, for
nine months, with all of it: the tiredness, the waiting, the not knowing. "The
Word was made Flesh" happened in a womb. Dani is praying the Angelus from
inside the same experience.

### At evening — a short examen
*`kind: daily`, `source: original`*

Five questions. No writing required.

1. Where was there kindness today — mine, or someone else's?
2. What did my body ask for that I ignored?
3. What frightened me?
4. What am I grateful for, even if it is very small?
5. Lord, what do we need tomorrow?

> Thank you for today, and for carrying us both through it.
> Forgive me what I got wrong. I am too tired to list it; you know it all.
> Bless Nico. Bless our cub. Let us sleep.
> Amen.

### In the night — for the hours when sleep will not come
*`kind: daily`, `source: original`*

> Lord, it is late and I am awake again.
>
> I will not ask for sleep, though I would like it. I will use this quiet
> instead: to be still with the one you are making, who is awake in here with
> me, turning.
>
> Hold the both of us through the dark. Let the morning be kind.
>
> Amen.

**And when even that is too much**, the oldest Marian prayer in the Church,
from third-century Egypt — short enough for any hour:

> We fly to thy protection, O holy Mother of God.
> Despise not our petitions in our necessities,
> but deliver us always from all dangers,
> O glorious and blessed Virgin. Amen.
>
> *— Sub Tuum Praesidium, c. 250 AD*

## 2. Prayers for the moments

`kind: situational`. These are the ones the app should surface contextually —
the nausea prayer when she logs nausea, the scan prayer the morning of an
appointment. All `source: original` unless noted.

### Before a scan or a test

> Lord, in a little while someone will look at our child and tell us what they
> see.
>
> I want to hear that all is well. I am asking for that plainly, because you
> know I am asking for it anyway.
>
> But if the news is not what we hoped, be in the room then too. Let us hear it
> together, and not fall apart, and know what to do next.
>
> Whatever they find, this child is already yours and already loved.
>
> Amen.

### After hard news

> I do not understand this, and I am not going to pretend that I do.
>
> I am angry, and frightened, and I do not have anything to offer you right now
> except that I am still here and still talking to you.
>
> Stay. That is all I am asking. Stay with us, and with this child, and help us
> put one foot in front of the other.
>
> Amen.

### When the nausea is relentless

> Lord, I am so tired of feeling like this.
>
> There is nothing noble in it and I am not going to pretend there is. It is
> just grim, and it is every day.
>
> Get me to the evening. Then get me to tomorrow. And let this be, in some way
> I cannot see from in here, the price of something very good.
>
> Amen.

### When she is afraid

> I am frightened, and I do not always know of what.
>
> Of something going wrong. Of the birth. Of being a mother and not being good
> at it. Of how much I already love someone I have not met.
>
> You knit this child together in me. You have not stopped paying attention.
> Take the fear — not away, necessarily, but take it from *me*, and carry it,
> because I have enough to carry.
>
> Amen.

### For Nico

> Lord, bless the man who is going through this beside me.
>
> He cannot feel any of it, and he worries in silence, and he is trying very
> hard at something no one taught him. Give him patience for the days I am not
> easy to live with. Give him the right words, or the sense to say nothing.
>
> Make him the father this child needs. He will be.
>
> Amen.

### For the baby

> Little one — cub, for now —
>
> you are the size of something I could hold in one hand, and you have already
> changed everything.
>
> Lord, guard the small unfinished work of this child. Every cell of it. Let
> the heart keep its rhythm and the bones grow strong and the mind be bright
> and the soul be yours from the first moment, which it already is.
>
> And let us meet. Please, let us meet.
>
> Amen.

### In thanksgiving, on a good day

> Today I felt well, and I noticed, and I wanted to say thank you before it
> passed.
>
> Thank you for this body that is doing something extraordinary. For food that
> stayed down. For the kick I felt this afternoon. For Nico. For a due date to
> count towards.
>
> Thank you. That is the whole prayer.
>
> Amen.

### Past the due date

> The day came and went and nothing happened, and everyone is asking.
>
> Lord, you know the hour. You have known it since before I did. Let me stop
> counting and let this child come when this child is ready.
>
> Give me patience for the phone calls. Give me sleep. And soon — let it be
> soon.
>
> Amen.

### At the very start of labour

> This is it. It is beginning.
>
> Lord, I am frightened and I am ready, both at once.
>
> Be in the room. Be in Nico's hands, and in Dra. Villafria's hands, and in
> every nurse's hands. Steady my breathing. Let me be brave, and if I cannot
> be brave, let me be carried.
>
> Bring our child safely through. Bring me safely through.
>
> Into your hands.
>
> Amen.

### The Memorare — for when the words will not come
*`source: traditional`*

The prayer to pray when you cannot construct one.

> Remember, O most gracious Virgin Mary, that never was it known that anyone
> who fled to thy protection, implored thy help, or sought thy intercession,
> was left unaided.
>
> Inspired by this confidence, I fly unto thee, O Virgin of virgins, my Mother.
> To thee do I come, before thee I stand, sinful and sorrowful. O Mother of the
> Word Incarnate, despise not my petitions, but in thy mercy hear and answer me.
>
> Amen.

## 3. A prayer for every week

`kind: weekly`, keyed to `week` so the app can pair each with the same week's
guide entry. All `source: original`. Each has an **intention** — one thing to
carry through the week — and a short prayer.

### First trimester

**Week 4 — *Intention: that this is real.***
> Lord, there is almost nothing here yet, and already there is everything.
> A few days ago I did not know. Now I cannot think of anything else.
> Be with what has only just begun. Amen.

**Week 5 — *Intention: for the hidden work.***
> Today something in this child is deciding what will become the spine, the
> brain, the nerves that will one day feel warmth and pain and being held.
> None of it is visible. All of it is yours. Guard it. Amen.

**Week 6 — *Intention: for a heartbeat.***
> There is a heart in me that is not mine, and this week it begins to beat.
> Lord, let it find its rhythm and keep it — every day, for a long life.
> Amen.

**Week 7 — *Intention: patience with my own body.***
> I am sick and exhausted and I look entirely ordinary, so no one knows.
> You know. Let that be enough this week. Amen.

**Week 8 — *Intention: for the days I cannot eat.***
> Lord, I cannot keep much down and I am worried it is not enough.
> You fed a whole people in a desert. Make what little I manage sufficient
> for us both. Amen.

**Week 9 — *Intention: for the ones we will tell.***
> Soon we will say it out loud and it will belong to other people too.
> Give us the right moment, and the right words, and joy in the telling. Amen.

**Week 10 — *Intention: for the tests ahead.***
> This week we could learn things we cannot unlearn.
> Give us courage for the knowing, whatever it is. Amen.

**Week 11 — *Intention: for bones.***
> Bones are hardening this week — the frame that will carry this child through
> a whole life, running and climbing and standing up again.
> Build them strong. Amen.

**Week 12 — *Intention: gratitude for getting here.***
> Twelve weeks. Lord, I have been holding my breath since the first day.
> Thank you for bringing us this far. Let me breathe out. Amen.

**Week 13 — *Intention: for the turning of the season.***
> The hardest part is behind me, they say. Let it be true.
> And if it is not, be with me in the next part too. Amen.

### Second trimester

**Week 14 — *Intention: for returning strength.***
> I feel like myself again, and I had forgotten what that was like.
> Thank you. Let me use this season well and not waste it worrying. Amen.

**Week 15 — *Intention: for Nico.***
> He cannot feel any of this. He waits outside it, and worries, and asks how I
> am, and I am not always kind in answering.
> Bless him this week. Let him feel part of it. Amen.

**Week 16 — *Intention: for the first flutter.***
> Somewhere this month I will feel this child move for the first time and know
> it for what it is.
> Let me notice. Let me not be doing something else. Amen.

**Week 17 — *Intention: for the body that is changing.***
> Nothing fits and I do not entirely recognise myself.
> Let me be gentle with this body. It is not failing. It is working. Amen.

**Week 18 — *Intention: for the scan that looks closely.***
> They will count the chambers of a heart the size of a thumbnail.
> Be in that room. Let them find everything where it should be. Amen.

**Week 19 — *Intention: for the senses waking up.***
> This child is beginning to hear, to taste, to feel.
> The first world is my body, and it is a poor and ordinary one — make it
> enough. Let the first thing known be that it is loved. Amen.

**Week 20 — *Intention: halfway.***
> Twenty weeks behind, twenty ahead.
> Thank you for the half that is done. Carry us through the half that is not.
> Amen.

**Week 21 — *Intention: for the kicks.***
> There is someone in here now, moving with intention, answering when I press
> back.
> Thank you for this conversation, which is the strangest and best I have had.
> Amen.

**Week 22 — *Intention: for the marks this leaves.***
> My body is keeping a record of this and will not give it back.
> Let me not resent it. It is the evidence. Amen.

**Week 23 — *Intention: for Nico's voice.***
> This child can hear him now — better than me, they say, because his voice is
> lower and carries further.
> Let the sound of his father be one of the first things this child knows.
> Amen.

**Week 24 — *Intention: for the threshold.***
> This week our child crosses the line where life outside me becomes possible.
> Lord, I do not want to need that. But thank you that it is there. Amen.

**Week 25 — *Intention: for the aching back.***
> Everything hurts in small, unremarkable ways.
> No drama, no crisis — just a body doing hard work every hour.
> Give me rest where I can find it. Amen.

**Week 26 — *Intention: for eyes that open.***
> This week the eyes open onto darkness, and one day they will open onto us.
> Let them see a great deal of good. Amen.

**Week 27 — *Intention: at the end of the good season.***
> The easy trimester is closing. I am grateful for it and sorry to lose it.
> Ready me for what comes. Amen.

### Third trimester

**Week 28 — *Intention: for the last stretch.***
> Three months. It sounds short and it will not feel short.
> Lord, give me stamina. Let me not wish away the days I will later miss.
> Amen.

**Week 29 — *Intention: for the things I cannot control.***
> I have read everything and prepared what can be prepared, and almost none of
> what matters is up to me.
> Take the rest. I am not able to carry it and this child at once. Amen.

**Week 30 — *Intention: for the room we are making.***
> We are assembling a cot and folding very small clothes for someone who is not
> here.
> Bless this house. Let it be a good place to arrive. Amen.

**Week 31 — *Intention: for the birth, still ahead.***
> I think about it constantly and I am frightened of it.
> Whatever kind of birth this turns out to be, let it be safe, and let me be
> met in it. Amen.

**Week 32 — *Intention: for turning.***
> This child is finding a position for the journey out.
> Turn them gently, in your own time, the right way round. Amen.

**Week 33 — *Intention: for broken sleep.***
> Awake again. This is practice, I know.
> Let me not waste the nights being angry about them. Amen.

**Week 34 — *Intention: for the people who will help.***
> Dra. Villafria, the midwives, the nurses whose names I do not yet know.
> Bless their hands and their judgement. Let them be rested that day. Amen.

**Week 35 — *Intention: for readiness.***
> The bag is packed by the door. It could be any time now.
> Let me be ready without being afraid. Amen.

**Week 36 — *Intention: for the advice.***
> Everyone has something to tell me, and all of it comes from love, and some of
> it is nonsense.
> Give me grace for the telling and confidence in what we have decided. Amen.

**Week 37 — *Intention: term.***
> This child is ready. Whatever happens now, they are ready.
> Thank you. Amen.

**Week 38 — *Intention: for the last quiet.***
> Soon this house will never be quiet in the same way again.
> Let me notice this week. Let me sit in it. Amen.

**Week 39 — *Intention: for the waiting.***
> Any day. Any hour. I cannot settle to anything.
> Teach me to wait, which I have never been good at. Amen.

**Week 40 — *Intention: for the hour you have chosen.***
> The date on the paper is today and it means nothing; you have known the real
> day all along.
> I am ready. Bring our child out into the light, and let us see each other.
> Amen.

## 4. The traditional prayers, in full

`kind: traditional`, `source: traditional`. These belong to the whole Church
and are given unaltered.

### The Hail Mary

> Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee.
> Blessed art thou amongst women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus.
>
> Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners,
> now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

**Worth noticing while pregnant:** "blessed is the fruit of thy womb." Half of
the most repeated prayer in Christian history is about a pregnancy.

### The Magnificat — Mary's own song, sung while pregnant
*Luke 1:46–55*

Mary prayed this while carrying Jesus, on visiting her pregnant cousin
Elizabeth. It is the prayer of one expectant mother to another.

> My soul doth magnify the Lord,
> and my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour.
> For he hath regarded the lowliness of his handmaiden;
> for behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed.
> For he that is mighty hath done great things to me, and holy is his name.
> And his mercy is from generation unto generations, to them that fear him.
> He hath shewed might in his arm; he hath scattered the proud in the conceit
> of their heart.
> He hath put down the mighty from their seat, and hath exalted the humble.
> He hath filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he hath sent empty
> away.
> He hath received Israel his servant, being mindful of his mercy.
> As he spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and to his seed for ever.
>
> Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.
> As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end.
> Amen.

### Blessing of a mother before childbirth
*Adapted from the Roman Ritual. Traditionally given by a priest — **worth asking
your parish for**, and commonly done in Philippine parishes on request.*

> **V.** Our help is in the name of the Lord.
> **R.** Who made heaven and earth.
>
> Lord God, creator of all things, mighty and awesome, just and merciful:
> you alone are good and kind.
>
> You delivered Israel from every evil, and made our fathers a chosen people.
> You have made this your servant fruitful, and willed that she should conceive.
>
> Guard her, we pray, and protect the child you have given her to carry.
> Bring her safely to the hour of delivery, and let her bring forth her child in
> safety and in joy.
>
> Keep her under your protection, that she and the child she bears may come at
> last to the everlasting life of your kingdom.
>
> Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
>
> *May the blessing of almighty God, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit,
> come upon you and remain with you always. Amen.*

### To St Gerard Majella, patron of expectant mothers
*`source: original`, in the traditional form*

St Gerard Majella (1726–1755), a Redemptorist brother, is the Church's patron
of expectant mothers and of safe delivery. His feast is **16 October** — which
falls at **8w5d** this pregnancy.

> O good St Gerard, powerful intercessor before God and wonder-worker of our
> day, I call on you and seek your help.
>
> You who bore all your own sufferings with patience, and who are the appointed
> guardian of mothers and of children not yet born: watch over me in these
> months, and over the child I carry.
>
> Preserve us both from all danger. Bring me safely to the hour of delivery.
> And ask for this child the grace of baptism and a long life spent in the love
> of God.
>
> St Gerard, pray for us. Amen.

### To St Anne, mother of Mary

> Good St Anne, you who bore the Mother of God and taught her, and who know what
> it is to wait a long time for a child:
>
> pray for me now. Ask for me a safe delivery and a healthy child. And ask that
> I might be, to this child, something of what you were to your daughter.
>
> Amen.

### To St Raymond Nonnatus, patron of childbirth

St Raymond's name — *Nonnatus*, "not born" — comes from his having been
delivered by caesarean after his mother died in labour. He is patron of
childbirth, midwives, and mothers in difficult deliveries.

> St Raymond, brought into this world at the cost of your mother's life, and
> patron of every mother in her hour:
>
> stand beside me when my hour comes. Ask that both of us come safely through
> it, and that whatever happens in that room, we are not alone.
>
> Amen.

**A note.** His feast is **31 August** — inside the window in which this child
was almost certainly conceived. Make of that what you like; it is at minimum a
good day to mark each year.

## 5. A novena to St Gerard Majella

`kind: novena`, `source: original`. Nine consecutive days.

**When to pray it.** Traditionally the nine days ending on his feast, **16
October** (so 8–16 October 2026, weeks 7–8). Equally: the nine days before a
scan that worries you, or the nine days from 35 weeks as the bag goes by the
door.

**Each day:** the opening, the day's intention and prayer, then one Our Father,
one Hail Mary and one Glory Be.

### The opening, prayed every day

> St Gerard, friend of mothers and of children not yet born, I come to you for
> nine days with one thing on my heart. Carry it to God for me, who never
> refused anyone who asked you.

**Day 1 — For a safe pregnancy.**
> Ask that these months pass without harm to either of us, and that what is
> being formed in me is formed whole and well.

**Day 2 — For this child's body.**
> Ask for a strong heart, clear eyes, sound limbs, a good mind — and that if
> any of these is not given, we are given instead the love to meet it.

**Day 3 — For this child's soul.**
> Ask that this child be God's from the beginning, baptised, kept, and brought
> at last home.

**Day 4 — For my own courage.**
> Ask that I not be ruled by fear in these months. I am afraid often. Ask that
> it not be the loudest thing.

**Day 5 — For Nico.**
> Ask for patience for him, and steadiness, and that he knows he is part of
> this and not outside it.

**Day 6 — For those who care for us.**
> For Dra. Villafria, and every nurse and midwife whose hands we will be in.
> Ask for skill for them, and rest, and good judgement on a long shift.

**Day 7 — For a safe delivery.**
> For the hour itself. Ask that both of us come through it, and that I am brave,
> and that if I cannot be brave I am carried.

**Day 8 — For mothers who are not as fortunate.**
> For every woman carrying in fear, or grieving, or alone, or who has lost a
> child. Ask for them what I am asking for myself. It is not right to ask only
> for my own.

**Day 9 — In thanksgiving.**
> For this pregnancy, wanted and given. For a due date. For every week so far.
> Thank you. Take these nine days and do with them what is best, which may not
> be what I asked.

## 6. The Joyful Mysteries, prayed as a pregnant woman

`kind: rosary`, `source: original` (the intentions; the mysteries themselves are
traditional).

Three of the five Joyful Mysteries are about pregnancy and birth. Praying them
now is not a stretch — it is the plain sense.

| Mystery | What happened | The intention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The Annunciation** | An angel asks a young woman to carry a child, and she says yes without knowing what it will cost. | For the courage to keep saying yes to this, on the days I would rather not. |
| **2. The Visitation** | Pregnant Mary travels to her pregnant cousin Elizabeth, and the child in Elizabeth leaps. | For the women around me who are carrying too, and for the ones who have helped me. Let me be an Elizabeth to somebody. |
| **3. The Nativity** | A birth, far from home, in poor conditions, and it goes ahead anyway. | For a safe delivery — and for peace about the parts of it I cannot plan. |
| **4. The Presentation** | The parents bring their baby to the temple, and are told plainly that a sword will pierce her heart. | For the love that is already frightening. Let me not be undone by how much this will matter. |
| **5. The Finding in the Temple** | Three days of searching for a lost child, and a mother who does not understand her son's answer. | For the whole long road after the birth, which I cannot picture yet. |

**A way to pray it when there is no time or energy for a full rosary:** one
decade a day, in order, five days a week. Or the Annunciation decade only,
every day. Nothing is lost by praying a little.

## 7. The Philippine devotions

`kind: devotion`, `source: original` (the prayers), with traditional context.

### Our Lady of Perpetual Help — the Wednesday novena

The Baclaran novena is one of the great devotions of Manila, prayed every
Wednesday. Whether Dani goes to Baclaran or prays it at home, **a fixed weekly
hour is worth more than an ambitious daily plan that collapses by week 20.**

> Mother of Perpetual Help, in the icon you are holding your son, and he is
> holding your hand — a child startled by what he has seen, reaching for his
> mother.
>
> I am carrying a child who will one day reach for me like that. Make me
> someone worth reaching for.
>
> You have never turned anyone away. Keep us both. Bring us to the day of this
> birth, and past it.
>
> Amen.

### Simbang Gabi — 16–24 December 2026, weeks 17–18

Nine dawn Masses before Christmas. Completing all nine is said to earn a wish;
more truly, it is nine mornings of getting up in the dark because something
matters.

**A practical note, gently.** Dani will be around 17–18 weeks — the second
trimester, the best window for it. Early Masses mean early starts, crowded
churches and standing for long periods. If she completes all nine, good. If she
completes four, that is also good. **The devotion is not improved by
exhaustion**, and a pregnant woman who sits down during Mass is doing nothing
wrong.

> Lord, nine mornings in the dark, waiting for a child to be born.
> I have never understood this season the way I understand it this year.
> Amen.

### Santo Niño — 17 January 2027, week 22

The Christ Child, and the most beloved image in Filipino Catholicism. A
devotion for a household about to receive a child of its own.

> Santo Niño, you were once what our child is now — small, and carried, and
> entirely dependent on a mother who did not know what she was doing either.
>
> Bless the child in me. Bless this house that is getting ready. Let there be
> laughter in it.
>
> Amen.

### Flores de Mayo — the whole of May 2027

The month of Mary, and the month this baby is due. **Dani will spend all of
Flores de Mayo at 36 weeks and over.** There is no better month to be waiting.

> Mother, this is your month and it is my last one.
> Walk it with me. Amen.

**On Lent.** Ash Wednesday falls at 25 weeks. Canon law does not bind pregnant
or nursing women to the fast, and Dani should not attempt it. If she wants a
Lenten discipline, make it something added rather than something removed —
a daily decade, a weekly visit, a kindness.

**The alignment worth pointing out.** She will carry this child through Advent
— the Church's own season of waiting for a birth — and deliver in the month of
Mary. The liturgical year will be describing her situation back to her for nine
months straight.

### A father's prayer, for the months of waiting

> Lord, I cannot feel any of this.

> Amen.

## 11. If the news is not good

`kind: grief`, `source: original`.

> **Build this carefully.** These prayers must exist — leaving them out abandons
> people at the exact moment a prayer book should be there. But **the app must
> never surface them on its own.** No "you might also like", no weekly rotation,
> no card on the home screen. They live behind a quiet, plainly-labelled link on
> the prayer index and nowhere else. Nobody should meet these words on an
> ordinary Tuesday.

### For a mother who has lost a child

> Lord, the child I was carrying is gone, and I do not know what to do with my
> hands or my body or the rest of today.
>
> I had a name. I had a due date. I had a whole life imagined and none of it is
> going to happen.
>
> I am not going to tell you this is alright, because it is not.
>
> Take this child, who was real, and who was ours, and who was loved from the
> first minute. Hold what I cannot hold.
>
> And stay with me. I have nothing else to ask.
>
> Amen.

### For a father, in the same hour

> Everyone is asking how she is. No one has asked how I am, and I would not
> know how to answer.
>
> Lord, I have lost a child too, and I am supposed to be the steady one.
>
> Let me grieve this properly and not put it away somewhere. Let me be a comfort
> to her without disappearing myself.
>
> Amen.

### For a diagnosis that changes everything

> We were told something today that we will be living with for a very long time.
>
> I do not accept it yet. I may not for a while.
>
> Lord, this child is still this child — still ours, still yours, still exactly
> as loved as yesterday, when we did not know.
>
> Give us what we need for the road we are actually on, not the one we thought
> we were on.
>
> Amen.
